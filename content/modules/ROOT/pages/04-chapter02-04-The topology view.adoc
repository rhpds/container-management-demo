= The topology view

== Know

The Topology view turns a cluster full of abstract resource objects into a live visual graph.
Every workload, service, route, and the connections between them are rendered on a single canvas — and the graph updates in real time as the platform reconciles state.

For most container orchestrators this kind of view either doesn't exist or requires a third-party add-on.
In OpenShift it is built in, requires zero configuration, and works the moment you deploy an application.

== Show

=== Demonstration: Scale and self-heal without leaving the graph

This demo uses the *Online Boutique* application deployed in the previous section.

==== Step 1 — Open the Topology view

. Navigate to *Workloads → Topology*.
. Select the project where the Online Boutique was deployed from the project dropdown.
. The full microservice graph appears: all 11 services are automatically grouped into a single *online-boutique* application card because they share the `app.kubernetes.io/part-of` label.
+
NOTE: This grouping is an OpenShift concept — vanilla Kubernetes has no notion of an "Application". Workloads there are isolated objects with no visual or logical grouping out of the box.

==== Step 2 — Scale a service live from the graph

. Click the *`recommendationservice`* node (or any non-critical service).
. In the context panel that opens on the right, click the *Details* tab.
. Click the pencil icon next to *Replicas* and change the value from `1` to `2`.
. Click *Save*.
. Watch the node's pod ring in the graph: a second segment appears within seconds as OpenShift schedules and starts the new pod.

**Talking point:** _"No YAML, no terminal, no `kubectl scale`. The same reconciliation engine that runs cluster infrastructure is now managing your application replicas — and you can see it happen live."_

==== Step 3 — Delete a pod and watch the platform self-heal

. With `recommendationservice` still selected, click the *Resources* tab in the context panel.
. Under *Pods*, click on one of the running pods and click on  *Actions -> Delete Pod*.
. Confirm the deletion and come back to the Topology view.
. Watch the Topology graph: the deleted pod's segment briefly grays out, then a new pod segment appears as the ReplicaSet controller immediately replaces it.

**Talking point:** _"This is continuous reconciliation in action. The platform's desired state says two replicas — the moment reality diverges, OpenShift corrects it automatically. No alert, no runbook, no human intervention required."_

==== Step 4 — Open the running application from the graph

. Click the *`frontend`* node.
. In the context panel, click the *Resources* tab.
. Under *Routes*, click the URL to open the Online Boutique storefront in a new tab.
+
TIP: You can also click the small arrow icon (↗) that appears on the top-right corner of the `frontend` node directly in the graph to open the route in one click.

**Talking point:** _"From the same view where you monitored the topology, you can jump directly into the running application — no copy-pasting URLs or hunting through resource lists."_

==== Step 5 — Scale back down

. Go back to the *`recommendationservice`* node, click *Details*, and scale replicas back to `1`.

This keeps the environment clean for the rest of the demo.
