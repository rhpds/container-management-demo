= Correlating signals with the Troubleshooting Panel

== Demo flow

=== Know
Frame this as the newest evolution of OpenShift observability: instead of three separate tools, one signal automatically points to the others.

=== Show
Click a firing alert and let the console surface the related logs and resources automatically, with zero manual LogQL.

== Introduction

xref:07-chapter05-01-Monitoring your cluster and applications.adoc[] and xref:07-chapter05-02-Centralized logging with LokiStack.adoc[] covered metrics and logs as two separate destinations: `Observe -> Metrics/Alerting` and `Observe -> Logs`. In the newest OpenShift releases, the *Cluster Observability Operator (COO)* ties these signals together with a *Troubleshooting Panel*, powered by an open-source correlation engine called *Korrel8r*.

Instead of manually jumping between an alert, a pod name, and a hand-written LogQL query, the Troubleshooting Panel lets you click a signal and see everything Korrel8r considers related — alerts, metrics, logs, Kubernetes resources, and network events — all scoped to the right time window automatically.

NOTE: The Cluster Observability Operator ships some capabilities as Technology Preview, and the set of available UI plugins can change between releases. Verify what is available and enabled on your specific cluster version before presenting this section live.

[NOTE]
====
*Pre-demo requirement:* The Cluster Observability Operator and all three UIPlugins (monitoring, logging, troubleshooting-panel) must be installed and in `Available` state before this section. See xref:00-initial-setup.adoc[Initial Setup] for step-by-step instructions. After applying the plugins, do a hard refresh of the console (Ctrl+Shift+R) to activate the Troubleshooting Panel UI.
====

== The value of correlation: before and after

To make the impact of this feature clear, set the context with what incident triage looks like *without* the Troubleshooting Panel:

[TIP]
====
*"Without this feature, correlating an alert to its logs takes multiple manual steps: you note the alert name and namespace → switch to `Observe → Logs` → write a LogQL query using the right label selectors → manually align the time range to when the alert fired → find the relevant log lines → go back to metrics to cross-reference. If you don't know the exact label names, add another round trip. This takes 5–10 minutes per incident, and it requires platform knowledge that not everyone on the team has.*

*With the Troubleshooting Panel, it takes one click."*
====

== Demonstration

=== Step 1: Navigate to a firing alert

. Go to `Observe` -> `Alerting` and open the `DemoAlwaysFiring` alert created in xref:07-chapter05-01-Monitoring your cluster and applications.adoc[].
+
If you would rather correlate a real, story-driven signal instead of a synthetic one, recreate the `bad-app` `CrashLoopBackOff` pod from xref:06-chapter04-03-Intelligent Cluster Troubleshooting - OpenShift Lightspeed.adoc[] — `KubePodCrashLooping` is a default platform alert and makes for a more relatable narrative.

=== Step 2: Open the Troubleshooting Panel

. Click on the firing alert.
. Click on the alert description, then open the *Troubleshooting Panel* drawer.
. The panel shows everything Korrel8r considers related: the owning `Pod` and `Deployment`, and — with LokiStack wired up — a direct link into `Observe -> Logs` pre-filtered to that exact pod and time range.

=== Step 3: Follow the link to logs

. Click the log link in the panel.
. The Logs view opens with the correct namespace filter and time range already applied — no LogQL required.

[TIP]
====
*"What used to take three tools, tribal knowledge of label names, and 5–10 minutes of manual correlation now takes one click. The platform follows the signal for you. In a production incident at 2am, that difference matters."*
====

== Wrap-up

This section closes the observability arc of the demo. The three tools together tell a complete operational story:

* *Monitoring* — metrics and alerting, built-in from day one, no extra infrastructure
* *LokiStack* — logs that outlive the pods that produced them
* *Troubleshooting Panel* — one click from any signal to everything related

Tie this back to the value proposition from the opening of the demo: less time spent context-switching between tools during an incident means faster resolution, and a flatter learning curve for engineers who are new to the platform.
